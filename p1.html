Understanding P, I and D Control

A PID controller adjusts the actuator signal based on the difference between the desired setpoint and the measured output.

P - Proportional Control

Proportional action responds to the current tracking error. Increasing Kp generally makes the system respond faster and reduces steady-state error. However, excessive Kp may increase overshoot and oscillation.

P = Kp × e(t)

I - Integral Control

Integral action accumulates tracking error over time. It can eliminate steady-state error for a stable step response when the actuator is capable of reaching the target. Excessive Ki may increase overshoot.

I = Ki × integral(e(t) dt)

D - Derivative Control

Derivative action reacts to the rate of change of measured output. It can improve damping and reduce overshoot. Excessive derivative gain may amplify measurement noise.

D = -Kd × d(measuredY)/dt (filtered)

Key Learning Outcome

P primarily affects response speed, I reduces steady-state error, and D can reduce overshoot and oscillation. Actual behavior depends on plant dynamics, gain selection and actuator saturation.
